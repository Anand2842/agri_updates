publications, notifications, websites, reports, and other secondary reference materials.
Given the breadth and dynamic nature of the Government support ecosystem, this playbook may not be exhaustive. While every effort
has been made to include a wide range of relevant initiatives, certain schemes, programmes, or opportunities may not have been
captured, may have undergone modifications, or may not be active or open for applications at a particular point in time. Several schemes
operate through specific application windows, calls for proposals, periodic notifications, or time-bound implementation cycles, which
may change from time to time.
The information provided in this playbook is intended solely for general guidance and knowledge dissemination. It presents a high-level
summary of schemes and initiatives and should not be construed as a substitute for the detailed guidelines, notifications, eligibility
conditions, or directions issued by the respective Ministry, Department, implementing agency, or other competent authority.
Readers are encouraged to consult the official websites, notifications, and communications of the concerned Ministries, Departments,
and implementing agencies for the latest and most comprehensive information, including eligibility criteria, application processes,
timelines, and other operational details.
While reasonable care has been taken in compiling and presenting the information, the authors and publishers make no representation
regarding the completeness, accuracy, or continued availability of any scheme or programme. Any decisions, applications, or actions
taken based on the contents of this playbook should be undertaken after appropriate verification of the applicable scheme guidelines
and requirements.

Objective Who It Is For
This Playbook consolidates Central • Entrepreneurs & Founders
Government schemes and incentives • Students & Aspiring Entrepreneurs
relevant to India’s startup ecosystem into a • Investors & Incubators
single, usable reference document. It is • Policymakers & Intermediaries
designed to help entrepreneurs find the right
scheme for their stage and sector.
How to Use This Playbook
1 Start with the Decision Tree. Answer 5 quick questions to find schemes suited to you.
2 Check the “What Do You Need?” Index for a need-based view (grant, equity, loan, etc.).
3 Use the Lifecycle Map to find schemes matching your stage of growth.
4 Explore the Summary Table for a structured overview of all schemes with key details.
5 Deep dive into Scheme One-Pagers for full scheme details including how to apply.
Focus Legend
Startup Focus Legend
● Startup-Specific: Startups are the primary or sole eligible beneficiary explicitly named in
scheme eligibility.
● Startup-Relevant: Broader eligibility (MSMEs, companies, institutions) but startups regularly
benefit and apply.

Appendix Definitions & Glossary
Key terms used throughout this Playbook
Term Definition / Explanation
DPIIT-Recognised A startup officially recognised by the Department for Promotion of
Startup Industry & Internal Trade (DPIIT) as per notified eligibility criteria.
Fund of Funds A fund that invests in other venture funds (AIFs) rather than directly in
(FoF) startups. Startups receive funding when their venture fund makes an
investment.
AIF (Alternative SEBI-registered private investment fund that pools capital from
Investment Fund) investors and deploys into startups/companies.
Credit Guarantee A government-backed promise to repay a lender if the borrower
(startup) defaults. Enables startups to access loans without pledging
collateral.
Grant Non-repayable financial assistance provided by the government. The
startup does not repay the money and does not give equity.
Equity Investment Capital provided in exchange for ownership stake (shares) in the
startup. The investor becomes a part-owner.
Convertible Debt A loan that can convert into equity at a later stage. Used when valuation
is uncertain at the time of investment.
Proof of Concept Early demonstration that an idea or technology works, typically in a lab
(PoC) or controlled setting. Pre-prototype stage.
TRL (Technology A 1–9 scale measuring how mature a technology is. TRL-1 = basic
Readiness Level) research; TRL-9 = fully commercial system.
TBI (Technology An institution providing physical space, mentoring, seed funding and
Business networks to early-stage technology startups.
Incubator)
Incubator / Organisations that support startups at early stages (incubator) or fast-
Accelerator track growth of already-validated startups (accelerator).
EMD (Earnest A security deposit required in government tenders. DPIIT-recognised
Money Deposit) startups are exempt from EMD on GeM.
SEBI Securities and Exchange Board of India.
CGPDTM Office of the Controller General of Patents, Designs & Trade Marks.
Startup-Specific In this Playbook: schemes where startups are the primary or sole
named eligible beneficiary in the scheme’s eligibility criteria.
Startup-Relevant In this Playbook: schemes with broader eligibility (MSMEs, companies,
institutions) that startups commonly access and benefit from.
